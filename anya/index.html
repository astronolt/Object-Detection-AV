How Anya sees

The study model is a YOLO11s detector fine-tuned on the KITTI benchmark, 7,481 road images recorded from a car in Karlsruhe, Germany. It looks for the four classes of the study: cars (with vans), pedestrians, cyclists and large vehicles (trucks and trams). On KITTI's validation images it reached an mAP@0.5 of 0.685 over these classes, and it runs in about 2 ms per image on an NVIDIA L4 GPU.

The general road model is a YOLO11m pretrained on the COCO dataset and not trained on KITTI. It knows motorcycles, buses, bicycles, traffic lights and stop signs as well, and Anya labels a person sitting on a motorcycle or bicycle as a rider.

Good to know

  • The study model learned from German streets seen from a car. On Nigerian roads it has never seen okadas, kekes or danfos, so it may miss them or give them the nearest class it knows.
  • Camera height matters: footage from a car or a pedestrian's eye level works far better than drone or rooftop views.
  • Detections are not perfect. A score next to each box shows how confident the model is.
  • Nothing you upload is kept: each file is deleted as soon as the result has been sent.
Anya is the demonstration for the MSc study “Real-Time Object Detection for Autonomous Vehicle Perception”, University of Lagos, 2026. It is not part of the study's pre-registered evaluation.
